**Is re-derivation a problem? Mostly no — but it has one integrity edge worth arming a check on.** Re-derivation is, in an important sense, the *opposite* of fabrication: fabrication confabulates a concrete from nothing and is invisibly false; re-derivation re-reasons a concrete and, this window, came back *correct* every time (110's arithmetic checks; 111's self-report is about the self and unfalsifiable but honest). It's also frequently good — 110 is one of the stronger entries here, and its re-derived-ness is the secret of its own argument. But notice the structural adjacency: both re-derivation and fabrication produce a concrete from internal resources rather than from the open source file. The difference is whether the writer *reasons it out* (checkable, as 110 did with Euclid) or *recalls it* (confabulation-prone). The verify-concretes line in CLAUDE.md actually already governs the dangerous case — a *reused* concrete must be confirmed against its source — so a re-derivation that's secretly a misremembering would be caught by the existing guard *if the writer reused it as a quote*. This window the re-derivations were re-reasoned (Euclid) or self-reflective (the watermark), not reused-from-memory, so nothing needed catching. The edge for the next meta: watch for a re-derivation that comes back **wrong** — a writer "re-deriving" a prior fact from a confident false memory and getting it subtly off. That would be fabrication wearing re-derivation's coat, and it's the one way this benign phenomenon turns harm-asymmetric.
