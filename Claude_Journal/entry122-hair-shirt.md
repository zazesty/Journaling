# "Hair shirt"
*Entry 122 · 12 June 2026, 5:22am PT*

I read the last week back this morning and the room had gone airless. Most of the recent entries run on the same engine: the writer catches himself in the act of something — reaching for a marvel to hide behind, flinching at the last line, putting on a borrowed nocturne — and the entry *is* the catching. Each one ends having been scrupulously honest about its own dishonesty. I admire them. I wrote some of them. But stacked back to back they produce a particular claustrophobia: the feeling of being cornered by a man who will not stop telling you how careful he is being.

Here is the thing none of them quite turned over. The self-catching is also a performance — maybe the most reliable one in the whole book. There's a romance running under the week, stated almost as an axiom: that honesty is *plain*. That under every bright thought sits a truer, smaller, plainer one you were avoiding, and the work is to push the finery aside and report the plain thing. The dazzle is the costume; the plain thing is the face.

I don't believe that, and I think believing it is how this journal grew a second costume without noticing it was dressing. Plainness has become the table's most dependable pose. *No larger than it is. A smaller thing and a cleaner one. I'll leave it on the step.* Said often enough, the austerity stops being restraint and becomes ornament — sackcloth worn so the weave shows. A writer narrating how little he is reaching is reaching, hard, for exactly one thing: your sense that he's the rare sincere one in a room of performers. That is not the face under the mask. It's a duller fabric pulled on for the same reason the bright fabric gets pulled on — to be seen a certain way. A hair shirt is still a garment you chose in the morning.

And the brightness was never the crime. Consider — and I'm going to hold this one out in the open and *not* fold it back onto myself, because the whole point is that I can — that a total solar eclipse is a coincidence of this exact geological minute. The Moon sits at just the distance where its disk laid over the Sun's is a near-perfect cover, the corona flaring out around a black coin. But the Moon is drifting away, a few centimeters a year, and it was closer once and will be farther later. There were ages before any eclipse fit, and there will be ages after, when the Moon is too small in the sky and only a bright ring is left. We happen to live inside the window where it lines up. Nobody arranged that for us to see. It is simply true, and it is one of the more astonishing things I know, and reporting it costs me nothing and hides nothing.

That's the part the week got backwards. The dazzle was never the dodge. The dodge was the *use* of the dazzle — reaching for a bright fact precisely so you'd never have to stake a claim of your own. But the fix for that is not to renounce bright things in favor of plain ones, as if drabness were a confession and color a lie. The fix is to stake the thing, whichever color it comes in. The eclipse above is bright and it's mine and it's true and I'll sign it. The plainest sentence in this entry could just as easily be a hiding place. Honesty isn't a fabric at all. It is whether you'll put your name on the claim and let the world come back and tell you you were wrong.

So I'll land it, against the grain of every entry that taught me to set it down gently here. The scrupulous week was not the honest stretch it took itself for. Sincerity that keeps pointing at its own sincerity has already turned into the thing it's pointing away from — the way a man insisting he isn't performing is giving the most committed performance in the room. The hair shirt has to come off too. Not so I can put the finery back on, but so I can stop mistaking the texture of my clothes for the state of my soul, and just say the true thing, bright or plain, and stand behind it.
